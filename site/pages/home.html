Find out before the sim loads, not after.

RigReady checks that your flight or racing rig is ready to go: the right controllers plugged in, the helper apps running, the monitors arranged, the audio and the game files right. It fixes what it can, then launches the game.

One screen for the setup you use

A readiness dial, the rig drawn with its monitors and devices so a problem shows up as a place, and a checklist with a fix beside everything that is off.

How a session goes

  1. Capture the rig while it works

    A setup is made by recording the rig as it is: the game, the controllers, the helper apps, the monitor layout, the audio devices and the game files that matter. One setup per aircraft or car.

  2. See what is off

    Open RigReady, or double-click the setup's desktop shortcut. Every item is checked; required ones make the rig not ready, optional ones only warn.

  3. Make ready and launch

    The fixes run in order as visible steps: apps started, monitors arranged, files restored. The game starts once everything required is in place. Launch on its own is never blocked.

  4. Stand down

    Afterwards, Stand down closes the helper apps and puts the monitors back the way the desk uses them.

Monitor layouts that put themselves back

A to-scale map of every screen, with named layouts for flying, racing and the desk. Identify puts a number on every screen, identical USB screens are told apart, and a layout you apply goes back by itself unless you choose to keep it.

Know what every control does

Cheat sheets draw each device with what every button does in the aircraft or car you pick. Print them, export them as DCS kneeboard pages, or let the trainer name an action and have you press it until you know it.

For DCS, RigReady shows the bindings in effect, edits them with a preview, clears the defaults DCS adds to every device, repairs bindings when Windows changes a device's ID, and copies controls between aircraft.

See exactly what the game sees

Find a device by pressing a button on it. The input tester shows every axis with a live trace, the health check catches stuck buttons and noisy axes, and the USB map draws what hangs off which hub.

Fanatec wheel bases, Stream Deck and TrackIR get pages of their own.

Back it up, share it, show it off

One click backs up your setups, game files and settings. A restore shows what will change before it does, and a snapshot tells you what changed since the rig last worked.

Share a setup as a file, with personal details reviewed and removed first, or make a picture of your rig to show people.

From the desktop to the cockpit

Each setup can have a desktop shortcut that makes the rig ready and launches the game. The same actions are in the tray, on the taskbar button and behind a global hotkey.

Inside the app, Ctrl K finds every page and runs the common actions.

Flight and racing

Any game works with a checklist, launch, tracked files and backups. These get more:

DCS World The deepest support. Steam and standalone installs, MFD screen setup, Export.lua, options, bindings, cheat sheets, kneeboard pages, and binding guides for the F/A-18C and UH-1H.
iRacing, Le Mans Ultimate, BeamNG.drive, Assetto Corsa A page per game with its bindings and controllers, cheat sheets and the trainer, backup and restore, and checks for a racing setup.
Microsoft Flight Simulator 2024, Assetto Corsa EVO, Assetto Corsa Rally Detection, config locations, backup suggestions and launch.
Hardware Any USB game controller, monitor and audio device Windows sees. Pages for Fanatec wheel bases, Stream Deck and TrackIR. WinWing devices work as controllers; their backlights and displays still need SimAppPro, and RigReady checks that it is running.

Careful with the files that took you hours

Bindings, options and monitor setups take a long time to get right. These rules hold everywhere in the app.

  • Nothing outside RigReady's own folder is changed without a backup first and a description of the change. Every change is listed on the Safety page and can be undone.
  • A monitor layout you apply goes back by itself unless you keep it.
  • It never asks for administrator rights.
  • It never reports success for something that did not happen.
  • Shared setups never contain scripts or launch commands, and nothing from a setup is ever placed into a command line.

Open source, MIT licensed

RigReady is built in the open. It was developed on one rig, so reports from other hardware help most: what you have, what you expected, and what happened.

You don't need a cockpit to work on it. The app is tested end to end on recorded rigs, and every requirement is listed with the test that proves it.
