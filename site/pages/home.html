Less time on rig chores. More time flying and racing.

RigReady is a free Windows app for flight and racing sim rigs. It does the work around the game: setting up your controls and screens, learning what every button does, getting everything running before you launch, and putting your desk back after.

{{HERO_ACTIONS}}

  1. Set it up

    Bind your controls for DCS and iRacing, with a preview first and Undo after, helped by binding guides for DCS aircraft and iRacing car classes. It also sets up monitor layouts, DCS's MFD screens and Export.lua without SimAppPro, and iRacing's control profiles.

    Bindings in the manual

  2. Learn it

    Cheat sheets show what every control on your stick, throttle, wheel or button box does, on paper or as DCS kneeboard pages. The trainer names an action and you press its control. The overlay lists each button you press and what it does in that aircraft or car.

    The trainer in the manual

  3. Launch

    One check before the session covers the stick and throttle or wheel and pedals, helper apps, monitors and MFD screens, audio and game files. Make ready fixes what is off, in order, and starts the game. When a control acts up, the input tester, health check and USB map help find out why.

    The Play screen in the manual

  4. Put it away

    After the session, Stand down closes the helper apps and puts your monitors back in the desk layout. It can run by itself when the game closes, after a countdown you can cancel.

    Stand down in the manual

Every change RigReady makes outside its own folder is backed up first and can be undone on the Safety page. When Windows gives a controller a new ID, device ID repair carries its bindings over, so the game still finds it.

Before a session

Ready to launch

A setup records how the rig looks when it works. Before each session RigReady checks the rig against it, fixes what is off, and launches the game. Launch on its own is never blocked, only warned.

  1. Capture the rig while it works

    The game, controllers, helper apps, monitor layout, audio devices and the game files that matter. One setup per aircraft or car.

  2. See what is off

    Open RigReady or double-click the setup's desktop shortcut. Required items make the rig not ready; optional ones only warn.

  3. Make ready and launch

    Apps are started, monitors arranged and files restored as visible steps. The game starts once everything required is in place.

  4. Stand down

    Afterwards the helper apps close and the monitors go back to the desk layout.

  • Monitor layouts that put themselves back

    Named layouts for flying, racing and the desk. A layout you apply goes back by itself unless you keep it. Monitors and audio

  • Start without opening the window

    A desktop shortcut per setup, the tray, the taskbar button and a hotkey. Inside the app, Ctrl K runs any action. Starting without the window

  • Flying and racing on one PC

    A setup for the F/A-18C and another for iRacing, each with its own devices, apps and layout. Setups

Setting up

Controls, set up and saved

Setting up bindings for a rig full of controllers takes hours. RigReady helps you get them right, shows what every control does, and keeps a copy so a reinstall or a new PC does not mean starting again.

  • Capture a setup

    Record the controllers, apps, layout and audio as they are now, then edit. No form to fill in. Capture

  • Cheat sheets

    Each device drawn with what every control does, for DCS aircraft and the racing games. A controller with no drawing gets a sheet from your own photo of it. Print it or export it as a DCS kneeboard page. Cheat sheets

  • Back up and restore

    One click backs up setups, game files and settings. A restore shows what will change before it does. Backups

  • Share it

    Share a setup as a file with personal details reviewed and removed, or make a picture of the rig to show people. Sharing

Moving to a new PC

  1. Back up on the old PC and export the file.
  2. Install the games and tools, plug in the controllers.
  3. Restore the file. Each item shows where it lands.
  4. Move the device IDs so DCS finds its bindings again.
  5. Run the checks and fix what is left.

When something is off

See what the rig is doing

A view that creeps, a button that does nothing, a panel the game no longer knows. These tools show what each controller sends and where it is plugged in.

  • Find a device

    Press a button on it and RigReady opens it: its IDs, its port, and a name you choose. Devices

  • Health check

    Hands off for ten seconds: stuck buttons, inputs that fire by themselves and noisy axes. Health check

  • USB map

    Which hub each device hangs off, how close you are to the limits, and what to unplug. USB map

  • New device IDs

    When Windows gives a controller a new ID, RigReady moves the DCS bindings, or points iRacing's files at it (the iRacing repair is not yet tried on real hardware). Device IDs

Wheel bases of every major brand, Stream Deck and TrackIR have pages of their own, and RigReady notices when HidHide is hiding a device. In the manual: Hardware.

Optional AI help with setup and bindings

AI is off until you turn it on, and nothing else in RigReady needs it. It comes in two forms, and either works without the other. Either way, every change is backed up first and can be undone on the Safety page.

With your own key from Anthropic, OpenAI, Google or a local model

The built-in assistant works anywhere in RigReady: it finds where your helper programs are installed, proposes the checks and launch steps of a ready check, and writes scripts you read in full before saving. In the binding guide it suggests a plan for your controllers and explains any action. You see what will be sent before each request, it goes only to the provider you chose, and nothing is saved until you press Save. With Ollama or LM Studio it never leaves your PC.

With an assistant you already use

Connect Claude Desktop, Claude Code, Cursor or another MCP client. It can check the rig, read the manual and propose changes, and by default RigReady asks you before anything is changed or launched. What it reads goes to that assistant, never to RigReady's author.

Flight and racing

Any game works with a checklist, launch, tracked files and backups, and one RigReady does not know can be added on the Games page, to be found, launched and backed up like the rest. These get more; the manual has a section on DCS World and a page per game:

DCS World The deepest support. Steam and standalone installs, MFD screen setup, Export.lua, options, cheat sheets, kneeboard pages, and twenty binding guides, from the F/A-18C, F-16C and A-10C to the Ka-50, UH-1H and P-51D. Bindings are read from DCS's own files, so there is nothing to import: edit them with a preview, clear the defaults DCS adds to every device, and copy controls between aircraft.
iRacing Bindings changed from RigReady with a preview, one button or key at a time; axes and hat switches are still bound in iRacing. How it keeps your PC safe

RigReady reads your hardware and changes game files, so it should say exactly what it does. These rules hold everywhere in the app.

The details, with the checks behind each one, are on the security page of the manual and in SECURITY.md on GitHub.

  • It installs for your Windows user only and never asks for administrator rights: not to install, not to run, not for a fix or an update.
  • Every change to a game or tool file is backed up first. Each one is listed on the Safety page, where it can be undone.
  • RigReady itself sends only two things off your PC: the update check to GitHub, and AI requests you send yourself, with your own key, to the one AI provider you chose. An assistant you connect over MCP reads only what you allow, and sends it wherever that assistant runs. There is no account, no telemetry and no crash reporting.
  • A shared setup never carries scripts or launch commands. An imported file is checked for size and cannot write outside the game and document folders.
  • Updates come only from the project's GitHub releases, are checked against their published checksum before they install, and never install while a game is running. A security fix is announced on every screen.
  • A monitor layout you apply goes back by itself unless you keep it.
  • It never reports success for something that did not happen.
  • The source is open, the whole app was reviewed for security before version 2.0, and a problem can be reported privately through the project's Security tab on GitHub. Fixes are named in the release notes.
